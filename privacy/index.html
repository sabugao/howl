Privacy

Privacy Policy for Howl

Effective date: September 24, 2026

Howl is an iOS app for sharing short audio clips. This policy explains what Howl collects and how it is used.

What Howl collects

Account information

When you create an account, Howl collects:

  • your email address (or, if you use Sign in with Apple, the email Apple shares with us, which may be a private relay address);
  • the handle you choose;
  • an optional display name, if you choose to add one, shown with your handle; and
  • an internal account ID used to connect your account with your posts.

You can sign in with Apple, or with a six-digit one-time code sent to your email address. Howl does not ask you to create a password.

If you use Sign in with Apple, Apple confirms your identity and shares only the details you allow. Howl does not receive your Apple ID password.

Audio posts

Howl collects and stores the short audio clips you choose to post. Your handle and posted audio are shown through the app as part of its audio-sharing features.

Voice filters are applied on your device before you post. The filtered clip is what gets uploaded.

Howl uses your microphone only while you record.

Transcripts

Howl may create a text transcript of a howl. Transcripts are used to keep Howl safe, including checking posts against our content rules, and to sort howls into categories and make them searchable.

Reports and blocks

If you report a howl, Howl stores the report: which howl, the reason you chose, your account, and when you sent it. We use reports to review content and enforce our Terms of Service. The person you report is not told who reported them.

If you block an account, Howl stores that block so neither of you sees the other’s howls. The blocked person is not told.

How Howl uses this information

Howl uses this information to:

  • create and operate your account;
  • send one-time sign-in codes and essential service emails;
  • store, display, and play the audio you post;
  • maintain and protect the service; and
  • respond when you contact us.

Howl does not sell your personal information. Howl has no ads, does not use third-party analytics, and does not track you across other companies’ apps or websites.

Bot and abuse protection

To protect sign-in from automated abuse, Howl uses Cloudflare Turnstile on the email sign-in screen. Turnstile may process technical signals from your device and connection, such as IP address and browser or device characteristics, to tell people from bots. Cloudflare handles this data under its own privacy terms.

Service providers

Howl uses:

  • Supabase for authentication, database hosting, and audio storage;
  • Resend to deliver one-time sign-in codes and other transactional email;
  • Cloudflare, for bot protection on sign-in;
  • GitHub, to store encrypted backups; and
  • Apple, if you choose Sign in with Apple.

They process information for these services on Howl’s behalf and have their own privacy and security terms.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account inside the Howl app. This removes your account and its associated user data from Howl’s server-side systems.

Howl keeps encrypted backups for up to 30 days so the service can be restored after a failure. Deleted data drops out of these backups within 30 days.

You can also contact us with a question or request about your information.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted here with a new effective date.
